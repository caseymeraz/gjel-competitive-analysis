The headline: 67% of all redirected internal links (1,980 of 2,965) come from just three template-level links that appear on nearly every page. Two are trailing-slash mismatches in the site template; one is the author byline pointing at a deleted author archive that 301s to the homepage. Three template edits eliminate two-thirds of the problem.

On anchors: the bigger risk isn't conflicting anchors — it's that your money pages are starved of descriptive in-content anchors. Your #1 organic page (/motorcycle-accident-lawyers) has only 6 in-content internal links. City hub pages are out-ranking their own practice-area child pages (e.g., /oakland ranks #10 for "oakland motorcycle accident lawyer" while /oakland/motorcycle-accidents — the page built for that query — has exactly 1 in-content link pointing at it).

Priority 0  Three template links = 67% of the problem

These links render on nearly every page of the site, so every crawl pass hits the redirect thousands of times. All three are one-line template fixes (header/footer CTA, attorneys link, and the post byline component).

/contact-us/ "Free Case Evaluation" 966 301 → /contact-us Change template href to /contact-us (drop trailing slash)

/attorneys/ "View All Attorneys" 472 301 → /attorneys Change template href to /attorneys (drop trailing slash)

The byline is also an anchor-text problem. Because /author/andy 301s to the homepage, Google currently sees ~540 in-content links with the anchor "Andrew (Andy) R. Gillin" consolidating on your homepage. Combined with 48 "accident attorneys" nav anchors, the homepage's internal anchor profile is a person's name — not what you want the homepage to rank for, and pure waste when a dedicated /andrew-gillin bio page exists. Repointing the byline fixes a redirect issue and an E-E-A-T/anchor issue in one edit.

Priority 3  760 links point at deleted posts that dump to /blog

239 old blog/news URLs now 301 to the /blog hub. Google treats "redirect to a category hub" as a soft 404 — the equity largely evaporates, and every anchor pointing there ("distracted driving", "settlement calculators", "Geico Insurance"…) sends its signal into a void. 123 of these links are in-content editorial links.

  • In content (123 links): replace each link with the closest living page — e.g., the 41 links to /blog/can-you-trust-personal-injury-settlement-calculators (anchor: "significant limitations") should point at /personal-injury-settlement-calculator or be unlinked.
  • In "related posts" modules (~637 links): these are mostly auto-generated related-article widgets still referencing deleted San Jose / Stockton news posts. Regenerate or prune the widget data.

The XML sitemap has the same disease: 238 of the 1,320 sitemap URLs return 301 — and 221 of those are deleted posts redirecting to /blog. Google is being invited to crawl 238 redirects on every sitemap fetch, which erodes crawl trust in the sitemap. These are in the Rank Math-generated post sitemaps; the deleted posts' sitemap entries should disappear once the stale post records are cleaned up (or the redirected URLs excluded). Full list: data/sitemap-urls-returning-301.csv.

Anchor Text  What Google sees when it reads your internal anchors

We analyzed all 90,002 anchors, separating in-content editorial links from nav/footer boilerplate (Google discounts the latter). Three patterns matter for the pages you want to rank.

1. Money pages are anchor-starved, and city hubs are cannibalizing their child pages

Why Google is confused: when the Oakland hub collects 15 "office location" anchors and the motorcycle child page collects one link total, Google resolves "oakland motorcycle accident lawyer" to the hub — and ranks it #10, below where a properly supported dedicated page could sit. Fix: from each city hub, link every practice child page with its exact service anchor ("Oakland motorcycle accident lawyer"), and from practice pages link city children ("motorcycle accident lawyer in San Jose"). 10–15 deliberate in-content links per priority child page.

Overall this list is short — the site does not have a widespread anchor-conflict problem. The conflicts that exist cluster exactly where you'd feel them: the verdicts/settlements cluster and city hub pages.

3. 167 in-content links use generic anchors

"Read more", "click here", "learn more", empty/image links · list in data/generic-anchor-destinations.csv

Most cluster on case-result cards ("View settlement" x7 to different verdict pages) and blog teasers. Each is a wasted opportunity to describe the destination. Rewrite the templates so result-card links carry the case descriptor (e.g., "$195K bicycle dooring settlement — San Jose") — most of these are one template component, not 167 individual edits.

Healthy  What checked out clean

  • Zero internal links to 404s across all 90,002 links — genuinely rare and excellent.
  • Canonicals are clean: every crawled page self-canonicalizes correctly; no canonical points at a redirect (the 4 flagged in Ahrefs live in non-sitemap archive URLs).
  • No http:// internal links in the sitemap page set (Ahrefs found 8 in deeper archive pages).
  • Redirects are single-hop in all but 2 cases — no systemic chaining.
  • Ahrefs health score 89 (crawl of July 13, 2026, 8,779 URLs) — the issues in this report are the bulk of what's holding it down: "Page has links to redirect" flags 1,652 pages there, consistent with the 1,062 sitemap pages we measured plus archive/pagination URLs.
